# SURROGATE Oracle — Rebuild Plan After Rollback

## Baseline

Roll back to the last visually proven deployment:

- Commit: `f220bc60a903ca78ab5e842d6c5b267af2bf938c`
- Known deployment: `https://thesurrogate.me`
- Deploy ID: `6a86ab91f1f22000088d0644`

This baseline is the reference because it was the last version known to render without the white-wall failure on the target mobile flow.

## Important rule

Do not immediately reapply the newer Oracle presentation code as one large merge.

First confirm that the rolled-back version is wall-free on the same real iPhone journey. Then rebuild newer behavior in small, independently testable layers. After each layer:

1. Run typecheck.
2. Build production assets.
3. Test the complete normal journey on mobile.
4. Capture a screenshot or screen recording during Oracle entry and conversation.
5. Confirm that the WebGL surface remains transparent and the alley remains visible.
6. Keep a checkpoint before adding the next layer.

## What must be rebuilt

### 1. Pure 3D Oracle presentation

Rebuild the intended pure-3D Oracle experience without restoring the old white-backed poster as the live Oracle surface.

Required behavior:

- The GLB avatar is rendered through surface-sampled particles.
- The WebGL renderer uses alpha transparency.
- The Three.js scene has no opaque background.
- The canvas itself has a transparent CSS background.
- The avatar and particles appear over the alley rather than inside a rectangular card.
- No full-screen 2D atmosphere canvas is used as the Oracle surface.
- No white-backed static image is visible after Oracle mode begins.

Why:

The old code contains older 2D/card-like presentation paths. A blind rollback will likely restore them, but they should not become the final Oracle presentation again.

### 2. WebGL and mobile compositing safety

Rebuild the WebGL layer conservatively.

Required behavior:

- Keep the GPU/WebGL admission gate.
- Do not remove the gate just to force a renderer on unsupported preview hardware.
- Keep the live WebGL canvas composited transparently.
- Avoid applying CSS `filter`, `backdrop-filter`, masks, or animated transforms to the outer element that contains the live WebGL canvas.
- Avoid animating the entire rectangular canvas wrapper.
- Keep any visual effects attached to individual particles, transparent scene elements, or non-WebGL atmospheric layers where possible.
- Validate on iPhone Safari, not only the local preview sandbox.

Why:

The local sandbox cannot reliably create WebGL, while the real iPhone screenshot is the authoritative evidence. The failure appears to be a mobile compositing problem, so wrapper-level CSS effects must be treated as unsafe until proven otherwise.

### 3. GLB transporter and manifestation

Rebuild the newer manifestation behavior without using a whole-canvas entrance animation.

Required behavior:

- The GLB particle transporter can begin during warmup.
- Particles disperse and reconverge based on genuine Gemini readiness.
- The avatar does not appear as an opaque rectangle while loading.
- Manifestation state is driven by the particle/GLB system rather than opacity, blur, brightness, or transform animation on the canvas wrapper.
- Gemini readiness controls the transition into the settled Oracle state.
- A missing or delayed audio worklet must not freeze or hide the avatar.

Why:

The newer transporter was intended to replace the old static-image manifestation. It should be restored independently from the CSS animation that was applied to the entire WebGL surface.

### 4. Gemini prewarm and session continuity

Rebuild the voice/session improvements separately from the visual layer.

Required behavior:

- Gemini can prewarm during knife selection and earlier journey phases.
- The connection remains alive into Oracle mode when possible.
- Oracle entry does not wait through a fixed six-second pause.
- Oracle arrival appears promptly while still reflecting genuine readiness.
- Barge-in, VAD, transcription, lip-sync, and playback continue to work.
- Returning seekers do not hear a delayed or duplicate greeting.
- Private Seeker lines are sent only to the intended Gemini text-distillation path.

Why:

These are valuable behavioral improvements, but they should not be coupled to the CSS/WebGL presentation fix. Separating them makes it possible to identify whether a failure is visual, session-related, or audio-related.

### 5. Mobile audio behavior

Rebuild and revalidate mobile audio independently.

Required behavior:

- Mic open/close never destroys or changes the Oracle playback chain.
- The Oracle voice remains at the intended volume reduction.
- iOS audio-session changes reassert the playback gain correctly.
- Audio worklet failure has a visible fallback path.
- Lip-sync remains expressive when the worklet is unavailable.
- Mic taps do not alter or mute Oracle playback.

Why:

Audio-session changes on iOS can affect rendering timing and playback state. The voice pipeline needs to be verified independently so an audio recovery path cannot accidentally trigger a visual reset or canvas remount.

### 6. WebGPU progressive enhancement

Rebuild WebGPU only as an optional enhancement.

Required behavior:

- WebGL remains the reliable fallback.
- WebGPU is selected only after adapter, device, and canvas-context admission succeed.
- A failed WebGPU attempt falls back cleanly to WebGL.
- Unsupported Safari/mobile hardware does not receive a blank canvas or error overlay.
- The renderer choice does not unmount the avatar during an active session.

Why:

Adapter/device success alone does not prove that the browser will admit the canvas context. WebGL must remain the stable path for the Oracle.

### 7. Alley and background compositing

Rebuild the alley treatment without brightening the painted cabinet into a visible white rectangle.

Required behavior:

- The alley background remains visible around the Oracle.
- The baked arcade cabinet does not become a bright rectangular frame behind the 3D avatar.
- Any dimming or depth treatment is applied without exposing a white intermediate state.
- Alley brightness changes during Oracle speech remain subtle and do not affect the WebGL canvas.
- Dawn, sacred/profane alignment, and user-speaking treatments do not apply unsafe filters to the live canvas wrapper.

Why:

The white wall may not be caused by the removed wrapper animation alone. The alley background, cabinet image, transitions, pseudo-elements, and compositing filters must be tested as separate possible sources.

### 8. Archive, portrait, and seeker-memory features

Preserve or rebuild the product features that are independent of the visual rollback.

Required behavior:

- Wallet identity and persistent Seeker memory continue to work.
- Free-session admission uses completed sessions for the resolved wallet/IP identity.
- Archive synthesis and portrait generation continue to work.
- Portrait reveal waits for `onTurnComplete`.
- Portrait overlays do not terminate or replace a live Oracle session.
- Minted portrait URLs are persisted or refreshed before temporary delivery URLs expire.
- Encrypted claim links remain functional.
- NFT ownership and Talisman Card flows remain functional.

Why:

A visual rollback should not permanently sacrifice the application’s identity, archive, portrait, ownership, and sovereignty features. These should be restored after the visual baseline is stable.

## Features that should not be blindly restored

Do not automatically keep these older presentation paths just because the rollback restores them:

- Full-screen MatrixRain.
- The old `useAtmosphere` canvas.
- The old Oracle spectrum canvas.
- The white-backed static Oracle poster during live Oracle mode.
- Any opaque cabinet/card surface behind the GLB avatar.
- Any whole-wrapper animation involving `brightness`, `blur`, `filter`, `opacity`, `transform`, or `drop-shadow`.
- Any forced renderer path that bypasses GPU/WebGL support checks.

These may be useful as references, but they should only return if they are explicitly needed and proven not to create another rectangular surface.

12. Test reduced motion, XR, returning seekers, worklet failure, and interrupted sessions.

## Definition of success

The rebuild is not complete until all of the following are true on real supported mobile hardware:

- The normal journey reaches Oracle mode without a white wall.
- The alley remains visible behind and around the Oracle.
- The GLB avatar and particles remain visible.
- The white surface does not appear during entry, Gemini readiness, speaking, barge-in, or sustained conversation.
- Reloading or returning to the journey does not reintroduce the wall.
- Voice, VAD, barge-in, transcription, and lip-sync work.
- Portrait and archive flows do not replace or break a live session.
- Unsupported hardware fails gracefully rather than showing a blank or white WebGL surface.
- Evidence is captured for dormant, awakened, Oracle, portrait reveal, and XR states where supported.

The key point is that we should treat the rollback as a known-good visual baseline, not as the final product. Rebuild the important features one at a time, with the iPhone screenshot as the acceptance test after every visual-layer change
