You’re right. I was over-weighting the narrative symbolism of knife selection and under-weighting **interaction quality**. Get the permissions and capability activation out of the way *before* the knife becomes playable engagement.

The right flow is:

```text
What is here?
  ↓
Enter Alley
  ↓
Set the room — concise capability preparation
  ↓
Native camera / mic / motion prompts, if full presence is selected
  ↓
Alley
  ↓
Choose a knife — uninterrupted engagement
```

For a returning user, place the same step immediately after their return-journey choice only when they have not already completed or declined it for the relevant session.

## Make the moment painless

The key is not “ask everything by default.” It is **one intentional preflight choice**, then resolve every eligible capability before the alley interaction begins.

> **Set the room**  
> Full presence lets the Oracle see, hear, and respond to you.  
> Your browser may ask for camera, microphone, and motion access next.
>
> **[ Enter in full presence ]**  
> **[ Continue without ]**

On **Enter in full presence**, immediately, inside that button’s click handler:

1. Start/resume the audio context.
2. Request camera and microphone together via `getUserMedia({ video: true, audio: true })`.
3. Request motion/orientation if the browser requires it.
4. Record the actual result for each capability.
5. Enter the alley regardless, with fallbacks where something was denied.

The browser can request audio and video together in a single `getUserMedia` call, but its own native UI chooses whether to show one or multiple confirmation steps; the app cannot control or suppress that presentation.[1]

## Correct language

Avoid “sound + touch.” Say:

- **Sound** — starts with the interaction and can be muted later.
- **Motion** — allows the experience to respond to device movement, if supported.
- **Camera and microphone** — enable the Oracle’s full-presence interaction.

Use “haptics” only as an effect label, not a permission label. Browser haptic behavior is device/browser-dependent and generally doesn’t give you a dependable, permission-gated web capability to promise.

## Implementation principle

Have the `Set the room` primary action call the permission APIs without an intervening async transition, animation callback, route change, or modal close. Browsers require a user gesture for audio startup, and `DeviceMotionEvent.requestPermission()` resolves to `granted` or `denied` on browsers that gate it.[2][3]

Afterwards, show a brief non-blocking capability result—e.g., “The room is set” or “Entered without camera”—and land them in the alley. Keep a small `Room` / `Presence` control later so they can change or retry components without replaying onboarding.

That protects the knife interaction from becoming a technical interruption.
